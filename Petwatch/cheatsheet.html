Framing · say this first

It's an absence, not an error. The stories are internally consistent and would build cleanly. That is exactly what makes the omission hard to see - and someone in this room may have written them. Respect first, then the finding.

The 20-second version

The stories let the owner write a schedule and the watcher read it. But nothing anywhere marks a task done, and nothing travels back. I read them three times before I noticed.

The 2-minute escalation · in this order

The owner's job is unserved. Nobody wants a schedule; they want to stop worrying. A schedule you can't verify doesn't reduce anxiety, it itemizes it.
The watcher's job is unserved. Their fear isn't "what do I do," it's "what if I get it wrong and they blame me." No way to show they did it right.
It's a safety defect. Multiple watchers + medication are both in the brief. Two watchers, one read-only schedule, one diabetic cat: that's a double dose waiting to happen.
Then stop talking and show it: Prototype tab → "Feed a dog who's already been fed." The guard argues better than you can. "Jessie did it at 7:12" is the whole thesis in one screen.

The constraint · keep this distinction sharp

Registered-users-only is a rule I was given and obeyed, not a gap I'm criticizing. Say it plainly: "I think it's the riskiest thing in the brief, so v1 obeys it gracefully and starts counting how often it hurts." Obedience plus instrumentation - that's the whole posture.
