PetWatch - from user stories to a product worth trusting

Mikel Rosenthal · Product Design
4-hour exercise 5 documents · flows & maps · 1 working prototype AI-assisted, seams shown

The user stories describe a schedule that is written and read, but never done. Nothing in them closes the loop. Add a completion state and an attributed record, and PetWatch stops being a schedule viewer and becomes what both people actually need: evidence that the animal they love is okay, and proof for the friend who took care of it.

02

What the brief leaves open, on purpose

This is a well-written brief, and part of what makes it good is what it withholds. Seven places where the stories go quiet at exactly the moment a real product decision begins. I read each silence as the actual question, so here is each one and what I did about it.

The loopEvery story ends at "view the schedule"
Create, edit, view - and then nothing. No story lets anyone say done, and no signal ever travels back to the owner. That absence became the thesis of this submission: completion plus an attributed activity record is the smallest change that turns a publishing tool into a trust instrument. The full argument, with its counter-argument
Read togetherMedication + multiple watchers = a safety problem
The brief lists medication as a routine type and supports several watchers per pet, and neither story references the other. Combined, that's a double-dosing incident waiting for a diabetic cat. The guard is enforced at the database - a unique constraint on (task, date) - with the UI as the second line of defense, not the first. A race between two watchers resolves to a calm "already done, Jessie at 7:12," never an error. Try to feed Kenji twice
Unchosen"A web or mobile application"
The platform is left to me, so: responsive web first. The deciding user is the watcher, who never chose this product and shouldn't need an app store visit to accept a favor - and the invite flow already lives in email, where a link beats an install. The one capability that genuinely argues for native is push, so notifications are scoped as the first post-launch release rather than a v1 assumption. Release 2, PW-25
Whose clock?"Feeding times," with no timezone anywhere
The entire product exists because the owner is away - and the brief never says whose 7am a feeding time means. Times anchor to the pet's timezone, not the viewer's: one column in the data model now, versus a trust-destroying bug the first time an owner lands in Lisbon and a migration later. Data model, decision 2
Recurrence depth"Daily or weekly routine" - and no further
How deep does repetition go? Every other Thursday? The third Sunday? The trap is building a general recurrence engine for patterns pet care doesn't have. A task is stored as a rule - time of day plus days of week - and occurrences are computed at read time. Only completions become rows. No materialized schedule, no backfill job, no drift between the rule and the truth. Data model, decision 1
The constraintInvitees must already have an account
The one place the brief is emphatic - which reads as a test of what a designer does with a rule they'd argue against. I obeyed it: the refusal is honest, hands the owner a copyable message for a channel they already have, and creates no invite record for a non-user. And every blocked attempt is logged, so revisiting the constraint becomes an evidence conversation with product strategy instead of a matter of opinion. The registration constraint
UndefinedNothing says what success is
No metric, no goal, no definition of good. I proposed one: the share of scheduled care events confirmed during an active watch period - the closest measurable proxy for "the owner stopped worrying." Paired with the counter-metric that keeps it honest: batch-completion rate, the twelve boxes ticked at 11pm that would mean the record is fiction and the north star is lying. Success metrics

04

The calls I made, and where they're defended

A submission is a set of judgment calls. These are the load-bearing ones.

Added to scopeTask completion + activity record
The stories have no completion state - the owner writes, the watcher reads, nothing closes the loop. With two watchers and medication in scope it is also a safety defect. Smallest possible addition, changes the product category. the PRD, The gap in the current scope
Held looselyThe surveillance counter-argument
Confirmation could make a volunteer feel policed. Designed as the watcher's record, never the owner's oversight - and it's the first thing to validate, with a pre-committed decision rule and a cheap fallback. Research A1
RespectedThe registered-users-only constraint
I think it's the biggest risk in the product - and it's a stated requirement, so v1 honors it with the least-damaging refusal I could design, plus the instrumentation that earns the conversation to revisit it. the PRD, The registration constraint
CutMessaging, payments, RRULE, native apps, ratings
Each with a written reason and a trigger that would reverse it. Deciding what not to build is most of the job. Backlog - deliberately not scheduled
Engineered cheaplyRules, not materialized occurrences
Schedule occurrences are derived at read time; only completions are stored. Saves a cron job, a backfill horizon, and a class of sync bugs - design reaching into the data model on purpose. the PRD, Data model
Made calmNo red in the watcher's path
The watcher is doing a favor. Overdue tasks surface by position and quiet amber - urgency without accusation. Visible on the prototype's Today screen. Prototype
05

How the four hours were spent

Hour 1

Close-read the stories. Found the completion gap. Made the scope calls and the cut list. The only hour that couldn't be accelerated.

Hour 2

PRD, backlog, and research plan - outlined and dictated by me, drafted with AI, then revised hard. Edge-case sweep curated into the edge-case list.

Hour 3 – 3.5

Prototype: interaction decisions, build, and testing the guard, undo, and invite-refusal flows in the browser until they behaved.

Hour 4

This hub, the AI log, annotations, and a final honesty pass - the "what I didn't do" sections are as load-bearing as the rest.

What's deliberately not here: validated findings (no users were consulted - the research plan is how I'd fix that), a design system doc, full screen coverage, and an accessibility audit. Each is named, with its place in the sequence, in the backlog's closing section.
