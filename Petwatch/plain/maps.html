Flows, maps & the full screen surface

The prototype goes deep on a few screens; this page steps back and shows the whole product - the back-and-forth loop that makes it work, every path an invitation can take, how the data fits together underneath, and all twenty screens with an honest label for how far each got in four hours.

01

One watch period, both sides

This product is really two people and a routine between them. The owner's week runs down the left, the sitter's down the right. The highlighted boxes are the loop - the sitter marks a task done, the owner sees it and relaxes. It's the one exchange the original user stories never mention, and it's the reason the whole idea works.

02

Invite & membership lifecycle

Every path an invitation can take, drawn out - because this is where things quietly go wrong. The link expires. Someone opens it from the wrong account. Someone accepts twice. Each arrow in this drawing is a situation the app has to handle gracefully, and it's the picture the engineers and testers would actually build from. The dashed box is the one path the brief doesn't allow - inviting someone who has no account - and we count every time someone tries.

the happy path the constraint, met honestly every arrow = a QA test case
03

Object map

How the app's information fits together, in six pieces. Two of them quietly carry everything: Membership is the small table that lets one person own one pet and sit for another without ever switching modes - the role belongs to the relationship, not the account. And Completion is the only record of something actually happening; the schedule itself is just a rule ("7am, daily") that the app does quick math on whenever someone opens it.
