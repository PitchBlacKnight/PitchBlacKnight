Questions

Good to know.

Does insurance really cover massage therapy?

Many plans cover medical or therapeutic massage when it's prescribed by a physician for a diagnosed condition — back pain, injury recovery, chronic tension, and more. Coverage varies by plan, which is exactly why we verify your benefits before your first visit. Submit the verification form and we'll do the checking for you.

What's the difference between medical and relaxation massage?

Medical massage treats a specific diagnosed condition with a documented treatment plan, usually under physician referral — and it's the type most often billable to insurance. Relaxation massage is about overall wellbeing: calming the nervous system, easing everyday tension. Both happen on the same table, with the same hands. The intent and documentation differ.

Do I need a referral from my doctor?

For insurance-billed sessions, most plans require a physician's referral or prescription — ideally including a diagnosis code and recommended frequency. For self-pay sessions, no referral is needed; just book online.

What should I bring to my first appointment?

Your insurance card and photo ID (if using insurance), your referral if your plan requires one, and completed new patient forms — or arrive 15 minutes early and complete them here over a cup of herbal tea.

What is your cancellation policy?

We ask for at least 24 hours' notice to cancel or reschedule. Appointments are one-on-one, so a missed session is time reserved just for you. Late cancellations may be subject to a fee.

What should I wear? Will I be covered during the session?

You'll undress to your comfort level in complete privacy, and you'll be professionally draped throughout the session — only the area being worked on is ever uncovered. Your comfort leads every decision.

Is prenatal massage safe?

Yes — with a trained therapist. Renise uses positioning and techniques designed specifically for pregnancy. If your pregnancy is high-risk, please check with your physician first; we're happy to coordinate.
