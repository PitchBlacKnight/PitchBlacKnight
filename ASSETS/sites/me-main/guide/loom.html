00 — The brief

A design problem you solved. Not a tool demo.

Goji asks for a ~3-minute Loom showing how you solved a design problem, with actual work samples. The instinct is to record the AI demo because it's the shiniest thing you have. Don't. Answer the question they asked — then let the AI work land in the last thirty seconds, as the answer rather than the subject. Read this twice before you hit record

You already have everything

no new assets

The Pathfinder case study is built, illustrated, and live on your machine. You're not making a deck — you're scrolling a page you already wrote, and talking over it.

Why Pathfinder

not Edison, not FIBER

It's the only one with a real problem at the front, research you personally did, a decision you personally made, and a visible outcome. Edison was a team effort and FIBER is thinner on research.

One take

~35 min total

Setup, two throwaway practice runs, then record until one is good enough. Small stumbles read as human. A polished read reads as a script, and they can tell.

01

Setup — twenty minutes

Do all of this before you think about the words. Fumbling the mechanics is what kills take after take.

The screen you'll share
Loom, camera, sound
The one-screen problem

If you're recording on a single display, your notes cannot live on that screen — the recording will show your eyes tracking text, and it reads instantly as reading. Two fixes: put presenter mode on an iPad or your phone propped next to the camera, or print the five beats and tape them just under the webcam. Looking slightly below the lens reads as thinking. Looking sideways reads as reading.

02

The run sheet

Five beats. The green tag is what's on screen; the serif text is roughly what you say. Beats, not a script — say it in your own words, or it'll sound read.

0:0025 sec
Your face · page at the top
I'm Mikel Rosenthal. I want to walk you through one problem. At Avant, technology advisors were running six-figure enterprise deals out of their inbox — requirements in email threads, provider research in spreadsheets, quotes in attachments named final_v3_REAL dot xlsx. Nothing had a state. And the expensive part wasn't doing the work — it was re-finding it.
Don't scroll yet. Talk to camera for the first fifteen seconds so they meet you before they meet the work. The "final_v3_REAL" detail is your best line in the whole recording — land it, and let yourself be a little amused by it.
0:2535 sec
Click "Evidence" in the page nav
So I shadowed advisors through live deals instead of interviewing them about deals — fifteen minutes watching someone reconstruct a quote history from their sent folder tells you more than an hour of them describing their process, because nobody describes their own workarounds. They've stopped seeing them. And I ran a full interface inventory: five primary blues, three button shapes, seven sizes — in a tool where advisors present live revenue data to clients. Inconsistency reads as unreliability.
On screen: the BEFORE / AFTER audit diagram. Let it sit. Don't scroll while you deliver the "five primary blues" line — the number is on screen, so stop talking for half a second and let them read it.
1:0060 sec
Click "Model" — the five-state rail
Everything followed from one decision about nouns. The platform had been organized around tools — a fiber tool, a quote tool, each a destination you visited and left. I reorganized it around a single object: the Project. It has a client, a scope, an owner, and a state — and it moves through five of them, in order. Evidence gathered in any state stays visible in every state that follows. So at any moment the advisor can answer where are we and why this — which is the thing that was actually losing deals.
This is the beat that gets you the interview. It's the only sixty seconds where you're visibly making a design decision rather than reporting one. Slow down. Then scroll gently into "Product" so they see the real screens behind the diagram.
2:0035 sec
Click "System" — tokens and components
None of that holds without a system underneath it. I built Pathfinder — token architecture, component libraries, governance — and it carried the product from 2.0 to 3.0 without a redesign. Then we extended the same token foundation into a marketing library that 350-plus people shipped from without a designer in the loop.
Fast beat. You're establishing depth, not explaining it. If you want the 350+ on screen, this is where you'd switch to the marketing case study — but honestly, don't. A window switch costs five seconds and buys nothing.
2:3525 sec
Stop scrolling · back to camera
What I'd add to that today is speed. I build design system rules as skill files the AI works inside, and I use the Figma MCP server so Claude Code reads the real variables out of a file instead of guessing at them. Which means a client can get a real system inside a normal project budget — the thing that always used to get cut. Happy to show you that live any time.
Stop scrolling before you start this sentence. The close is delivered to camera, not to a webpage. And end on the offer — it's an invitation to the next conversation, which is the entire job of this video.
Press R for presenter mode

Full-screen, one beat at a time, with a timer that turns amber when you run past the target. Space advances. Run it twice out loud before you record anything — the second run is always dramatically better than the first, and you want the recording to be your third.

03

What kills these

Every one of these is recoverable if you know about it beforehand and unrecoverable if you don't.

Delivery
  • Reading. The single most common failure. If your eyes track left-to-right, they know. Beats, not sentences.
  • Narrating the screen. "Here you can see the dashboard" — they can see the dashboard. Tell them what it cost, why it changed, what you decided.
  • Scrolling while you talk. Motion competes with your voice. Move, stop, then speak.
  • Apologizing. No "sorry, this is a bit rough" and no "I know I'm going long." Cut it in the edit instead.
  • Trailing off. End on the offer and then stop. Silence at the end is confidence; "so, yeah…" is not.
Content
  • Starting with your résumé. Nineteen years, GE, BCBS — none of it belongs here. They asked about a problem. Start with the problem.
  • Making it a tool demo. If the AI shows up before 2:35, you've answered a question they didn't ask.
  • Credit inflation. Pathfinder is genuinely yours end to end — say "I" freely here. Save the "team effort" honesty for Edison, where it's true.
  • Running long. "~3 minutes" is a filter. At 4:30 the reviewer stops watching and notes that you can't scope.
  • Burying the decision. If a viewer can't repeat "one object, five states" afterwards, the take failed regardless of how smooth it was.
04

After you record

Ten minutes of finishing that most people skip — and it's visible that they skipped it.

01

Watch it once, all the way through

Not to critique the delivery — to check three things: is the audio clean, is every figure actually visible on screen when you reference it, and can someone who's never heard of Avant follow the story. If all three pass, ship it. Re-recording for tone is a trap; the fourth take is always worse than the second.

02

Trim the head and tail

Loom lets you cut the start and end. Remove the two seconds of you reaching for the mouse and the moment of silence before you stop. It costs nothing and it's the difference between "recorded" and "produced."

03

Title it like a deliverable

"Mikel Rosenthal — Product Designer — Pathfinder, one object and five states". The title is the first thing the reviewer reads and it should promise the specific thing the video delivers. "Loom for Goji Labs" tells them nothing.

Description two lines: the problem in one sentence, and a link to mikelrosenthal.com
04

Check the sharing setting before you send

Make sure the link works for someone who isn't logged in — open it in a private window and confirm. A Loom that asks the reviewer to request access is a dead application.

05

Reuse it

This recording is not Goji-specific — nothing in it names them. It's now your standing answer to "walk me through a project," and it works as a portfolio link for any application. Record it once, well, and it earns for a year.
