02

Fix these first — they break the story

Three things are currently wrong in the exact artifact you are using as proof. All are quick, and the fixes make the narrative better.

Blocker 1 · the provenance gate fails open

pathfinder/build/metadata.mjs --check crashes — it still looks for components/ks-button.js, renamed to pf-button.js. Worse, it exits 0 on the crash. As a CI gate it silently passes while doing nothing. This is the script that is your answer to Documentation as a Source of Truth.

  1. Update the component paths in metadata.mjs to the pf- names.
  2. Make it exit non-zero on its own failure, not just on staleness. A gate that fails open is not a gate — and that sentence belongs in the email.
  3. Re-run node build/metadata.mjs --check and confirm echo $? returns 1 when you deliberately edit a generated file.
Blocker 2 · naming drift in the anti-drift tool

All three skill files still say keystone after the rename to Pathfinder: skill/keystone-ds/, keystone-composer/, keystone-steward/. If he opens them, the artifact whose entire pitch is preventing naming drift contains naming drift.

  1. Rename the three directories and update every internal reference.
  2. Re-run the build so dist/pathfinder.skill.md regenerates cleanly.
Blocker 3 · decide about the 7 failures

The build reports 7 findings at severity=fail, including real contrast misses — text.placeholder at 2.45:1 where 4.5:1 is required.

  1. Recommended: keep them, and lead with them. A build that reports its own failures is worth more than a green checkmark, and it is the honest version of your self-audit story.
  2. Fix only focus-ring at 1.53:1 — you already tell the story that the button never picked up its focus token. Fixing it lets you say “found it, fixed it, here is the commit.”
  3. Add --strict to the documented CI command so he sees the gate exists even though you run it permissive locally.

Where You See This Going

“How do you think enterprise design systems need to evolve… what would you prioritize if you were helping modernize a large design system today?”

Deliberately no artifact here. Seven evidenced sections followed by one of pure point of view lands harder than an eighth diagram. The thesis: for fifteen years design systems were documentation for humans. Now every artifact has a second consumer that will do exactly what the system says. Prose rules cannot be followed by a machine; structured rules cannot be ignored by one. That does not remove judgment — it moves it upstream, so the decision happens once in the system instead of once per component.

  1. Give the modernization order as a numbered list: audit so drift is a number; neutral token source generating every platform including Figma; map components to the repo before generating anything; encode rules and inventory as machine-readable files; govern the exits with a real deprecation path and adoption measured per team.
  2. Explain why inventory matters most: it is what stops a model generating a fourth button beside your three.
  3. End on the failure mode: at enterprise scale AI's danger is not malformed output, it is duplication. The system's job is to make generating a duplicate harder than finding the original.
  4. Stop there. No summary paragraph, no restating. The last line should be the sharpest one.
05

Order of operations

Roughly a day and a half of work. Nothing here requires building something new — only fixing, capturing, and assembling.

  1. Send the holding reply today. It buys the time, and his answer to “which areas are time-sensitive” tells you where to spend effort.
  2. Clear the three blockers. Provenance gate, skill file names, focus-ring contrast. Perhaps two hours, and every one improves the story.
  3. Capture the terminal artifacts — A, F, I. Same window size, same theme, same padding. Consistency across screenshots is itself a design systems signal, and he will notice.
  4. Capture the Figma artifacts — C, D, G. Real file, real names, no cleanup.
  5. Build the two diagrams — B and A'. These are the only things you draw from scratch.
  6. Generate the doc page. Provenance header first, then the eight sections. Have build.mjs emit as much of it as you reasonably can — a doc that regenerates is the whole argument.
  7. Write the email last. Once the artifacts exist the email writes itself, and every claim will have something behind it.
  8. Deploy to its own URL. Separate from /guide, its own navigation, nothing else linked from it.
  9. Read the email aloud before sending. Anything you stumble over is a sentence you would not have said.
