Process · Brief to handoff

Put the hard thinking where it has the most leverage.

AI reduced the cost of many mechanical passes. I spend that savings moving implementation consequences, research questions, and QA thinking earlier, while the product is still cheap to change.

Question→ Decision→ Artifact→ Consequence→ Learning↺

The division of labor, plainly: the judgment calls are mine, the production speed is the machine's.

01 Frame

Start with the hole, not the deliverable.

The brief tells us what someone requested. It does not necessarily tell us what problem deserves to be solved. Before anything gets designed, everything in the room gets sorted into three piles.

Observed

What the evidence actually shows. The Pathfinder audit board. The PetWatch brief's own words. Nobody argues with a pile of screenshots.

Inferred

What we believe but haven't verified. Labeled as such, out loud, so an inference can't quietly harden into a fact.

Unknown

What we can't know yet. This pile writes the research plan. The most expensive unknowns go first.

02 Research

Spend research where being wrong is expensive.

Assumptions get ranked by three things: impact if wrong, current confidence, and the cost of learning. These are the standing PetWatch hypotheses, and they are still hypotheses. The design didn't resolve them, testing will. Pretending otherwise would be marketing.

AssumptionImpact if wrongConfidenceCost to learn
Completion visibility reassures the owner without making the watcher feel monitoredHighLowLow, diary study
Guided care increases a first-time caregiver's confidence at the moment of careHighMediumLow, moderated task
Snooze reduces anxiety rather than licensing driftHighLowMedium, longitudinal
Always-Know cards stay useful without becoming invisible wallpaperMediumLowMedium

AI can draft a research plan and enumerate what could be tested. It cannot tell you which uncertainty is worth the calendar time. That ranking is the actual work.

03 Alignment & scope

Models are very good at adding.

Senior product work is often subtraction. Alignment is a product artifact: a workshop's value is the decision it produces, not the sticky notes it consumes. The most useful decision is usually what we are not building.

Not building · PetWatch, verbatim from the repo

  • Not yetAuth, registration, password reset. Acknowledged in one line.
  • Not yetMulti-pet management. One pet: Hex. The scenario is the scope.
  • Not yetMulti-watcher interface. The domain model supports it; the UI shows one "already done" moment and stops there.
  • Not yetReal notifications, backend, sync. The event log models sync; nothing implements it.
  • NeverGamification. No points, streaks, or confetti. Completion should feel like relief, not a score.
  • NeverA MISSED state. Past-due is DUE with elapsed time. Verdicts belong to the owner, not the system.
  • NeverAI-generated medical content. Doses, techniques, and escalation thresholds come from the owner and the vet, attributed, verbatim. AI never speaks at the moment of care.

Source: NOT-BUILDING.md, a standing scope guardrail read by humans and agents alike. Its rule 2: "Do not add features to demonstrate capability. Restraint is a scored criterion."

Each entry carries a why, and the "not yet" rows carry what would change our mind. Scope is a decision, not a backlog accident.

04 Build

Working software is now part of the thinking.

The implementation gets to argue back.

Building something exposes consequences that a static mock politely hides. Two examples, one from each featured project.

Interface decision

PetWatch needs snooze. "Can't right now" should be a legitimate answer, not a failure.

Implementation asks

Fine. What state is the Care Moment in now? What happens when snooze expires? What if two watchers act? Whose timezone owns the event?

Product model

One small button revealed a full state model: upcoming, soon, due, snoozed, in progress, needs help, completed. That model, not the screens, became the source of truth, as an append-only event log with derived status and its own tests.

The point

This is why implementation should meet design early. The question surfaced while the product was still cheap to change.

05 Pressure test

QA belongs upstream.

An edge case is often a product decision we haven't made yet. AI can enumerate possibilities cheaply, which is genuinely useful. Judgment decides which are consequential, which can wait, and which should never happen.

Upcoming→ Soon→ Due⇄ Snoozed→ In progress⇄ Needs help→ Completed
two watchers act at once offline at the moment of care whose timezone owns 7:15 stale client state notification never arrives access revoked mid-stay duplicate completion

Each of these is answered, deferred, or explicitly ruled out in the PetWatch domain model. The timezone one has a rule worth quoting: the schedule belongs to the animal, so care moments anchor to the household's time, never the viewer's.

06 Handoff

Can the team continue without me?

The artifact isn't the handoff. The decision model is.

A successful handoff transfers intent, not files. For every rule that ships, the next person should be able to find six things:

Intent

What the rule is trying to protect.

Rule

The rule itself, stated so it can be applied without me.

Rationale

Why it exists, in one honest paragraph.

Implementation

The token, component, or pattern that enforces it.

Evidence

What we observed that justified it.

Change condition

What new evidence would justify changing it.

This is why the Pathfinder governance work matters more now, not less. A system documented this way is portable intent, readable by the next designer, the next engineer, and increasingly, the agent. If the team needs me in the room to understand the handoff, the handoff failed.

07 What AI actually changed

The disciplines didn't disappear. The seams moved.

"AI does the boring work, I do the creative work" is a false division. AI participates in research synthesis, prototype construction, enumeration, and implementation. Humans still participate in all of those too. What actually changed is where the disciplines meet: a designer can test a technical hypothesis, an engineer can explore an interaction, and a decision meets its consequence in days instead of quarters. The senior job increasingly includes keeping those accelerated loops coherent.

Historically

  1. Research
  2. Design
  3. Handoff
  4. wait
  5. Implementation
  6. QA
  7. Discover consequence

Increasingly

  1. Frame ⇄ Prototype
  2. Prototype ⇄ Implementation
  3. Implementation ⇄ Inspect
  4. Inspect ⇄ Research
  5. Research ⇄ Revise

What got cheaper

  • Ingestion and first drafts
  • Enumeration of variants and edge cases
  • Implementation scaffolding
  • Documentation and consistency checks
  • Routine maintenance

What that makes scarcer

  • Judgment and taste
  • Problem framing
  • Human understanding
  • Prioritization and editing
  • Restraint

This is an observed change in working economics, not a manifesto. And the honest ledger cuts both ways: the AI is sometimes confidently wrong, and it matters. In PetWatch it proposed a ticking countdown, a MISSED state, and compliance-toned copy. All three were rejected, and the rejections are documented in the case study.

08 Continuity

The tools changed. The question didn't.

Early Photoshop era→ Enterprise product work→ Complex systems→ Design systems→ Pathfinder→ AI-assisted development

I've retooled every time the medium moved. The enduring question has stayed remarkably stable: how do you use the new capability without losing the human judgment that made the work useful in the first place?
