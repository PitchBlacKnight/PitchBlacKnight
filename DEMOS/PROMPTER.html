01 · Opener (60 sec)

  • Feed the machine everything. Brief, research, emails. AI synthesizes in minutes.
  • My day-one job: find the hole. Absences don't pattern-match. A model won't catch them.
  • Rank assumptions by risk. Write decision rules before research runs.
  • Prototype beats an opinion. Build the real thing fast.
  • My hours go to scope cuts and taste.

"The test of my handoff is whether they still need me in the room. They shouldn't."

02 · The PetWatch story, four beats

Beat 1 · The hole

  • Brief looked like scheduling. Stories were consistent.
  • But: a schedule that's written and read, never done.
  • Nothing flagged it. That's why it's a designer's job.

Beat 2 · Hex

  • Hex is my real cat. Insulin twice a day.
  • Friend can remember "7:15." Doesn't mean they feel able to do it.
  • Their fear: "This is my friend's animal. I don't want to hurt him."

"A schedule transfers information. This has to transfer confidence."

Beat 3 · Two kinds of knowing

  • Care Moments: insulin at 7:15. Becomes due, gets done, resolves for both people.
  • Always-Know: Hex bolts through doors. No schedule. Never interrupts, never goes away.

"Some knowledge has a time. Some knowledge has a door."

Beat 4 · The refusals

  • No countdown. A countdown counts down to stress.
  • No MISSED state. Past-due is "due, 20 minutes ago." Verdicts belong to the owner.
  • No red anywhere. It shouts at a volunteer doing you a favor.
  • No confetti. Completion is relief, twice: "I handled it." "Someone handled it."
  • No AI at the moment of care. Instructions come from owner and vet, verbatim.

"Every one of those is a decision, not an omission."

03 · They'll ask about AI

"Doesn't AI make designers lazy?"

  • "Lazy designers get faster. Rigorous designers get more rigorous."
  • "Edge-case sweep moved QA thinking earlier. More rigor, not less."

"How do we know what's yours?"

  • "I keep an AI-usage log on every project. With the failures in it."

"Ask me about the red overdue styling."

"Tell me about the red styling." (they will)

  • "First pass at overdue was red and urgent. Confidently produced. Standard pattern."
  • "Wrong product. It shouts at a volunteer doing you a favor."
  • "Amber is the correction. That's the job: standing where the judgment goes."

"What if our team doesn't use AI?"

  • "Nothing in the package requires it. Generated docs, standard tokens, decision log."
  • "The skill file is a bonus, not a dependency."

04 · They'll ask about the two versions

"Why did you redo it?"

  • "The timed version answered 'did someone handle it?'"
  • "It never answered 'can I handle this?' That's the real product."

"Reconsidering your own shipped answer is part of product judgment."

"Isn't it cheating to go outside the timebox?"

  • "The original is still public, untouched. Four hours, unedited."
  • "The second version says on its face it broke the timebox, and why."

"What would you do next?"

  • "Run the research plan. Four open questions, ranked by cost of being wrong."
  • "Top one: does completion visibility reassure the owner without making the watcher feel monitored?"

"I won't pretend the design resolved those. Testing will."

05 · If they dig into v1 (own it fast)

Data model: ownership stored twice

  • "Yes. The exact bug class my PRD brags about eliminating for occurrences."
  • "Fix: derive owner from Membership. The reconsidered event log is that lesson, systematized."

Privacy fallback vs. the safety case

  • "The passive view protects the relationship by destroying the double-dosing guarantee."
  • "Fix: third variant. Passive for routine, itemized for safety-flagged."

"A fallback needs the same scrutiny as the primary. It's what you ship when the test goes badly."

"Where did the 60% metric come from?"

  • "Nowhere yet. Honestly."
  • "Should have said: working hypotheses, replaced after five real handoffs."

Retention metric

  • "90-day window on an episodic product. A happy twice-a-year traveler reads as churn."
  • "Measure 'used again on the next trip' instead."

Missing emergency vet number

  • "Cut by association when I excluded 'vet records.' Needs don't arrive in categories."
  • "Always-Know is partly that lesson."

06 · Land the plane

  • "One honest thing, because a process that only lists successes is marketing:"
  • "The AI is sometimes confidently wrong, and it matters."
  • "The workflow works because a designer stands exactly where the judgment goes."

"That's the job now. I think it's a better job than the one it replaced."
