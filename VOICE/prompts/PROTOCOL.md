# The capture protocol

The goal is not good answers. The goal is unedited speech. Anything that makes
you sound better in the moment makes the sample worthless.

## Rules for every session

1. **No notes. No screen. No bullets.** If you can see words, you will read them,
   and reading is a different motor program than talking. This is why the
   teleprompter failed: you were performing a document instead of answering a
   person.
2. **First take only.** Never re-record a prompt. The second take is a rehearsal
   of the first and it is already fiction.
3. **Move.** Walk, drive, wash dishes, pace the block. Divided attention starves
   the part of you that performs. You cannot self-monitor and parallel park.
4. **Talk to one named person.** Not "an interviewer." Pick an actual person and
   say their name out loud before you start. Your speech pattern changes when the
   audience is abstract, and abstract is where the borrowed voice lives.
5. **Overrun the prepared material.** Set a timer and keep talking until it goes
   off, even badly. Prepared answers run dry around forty seconds. Everything
   after that is you.
6. **Do not stop when it gets bad.** The mess is the data.

## Session 1: the failure set (20 min, 5 prompts x 3 min)

Failure narratives are the hardest thing to perform, which makes them the
cleanest sample.

1. The last time you were genuinely stuck on Petwatch. Start in the middle of the
   hour, not at the beginning of the project. What was on the screen.
2. Something you built and threw away. Why you threw it away, and what you
   thought at the moment you knew.
3. A time you were wrong in front of someone at BCBS or GE. What you said next.
4. The worst thing about how you work. Not a humblebrag, the real one.
5. Something you shipped that you are not sure was good.

## Session 2: the process set (20 min, 5 prompts x 3 min)

This is the question that broke you. Answer it five different ways without ever
saying the word "process."

1. Walk me through last Tuesday. The literal day. What you touched, in order.
2. Explain FIBER to your brother. He does not work in healthcare and he will
   interrupt you if you get boring.
3. What do you do in the first ten minutes of a new project, before you know
   anything.
4. How do you know when a thing is done. Not the ideal answer, what actually
   happens.
5. Where does the AI stop and you start. Be specific about a single file.

## Session 3: the opinion set (15 min, 5 prompts x 2-3 min)

Opinion produces the strongest idiolect signal. Rhythm, emphasis, and your own
metaphors surface here and nowhere else.

1. What everyone gets wrong about designing with AI right now.
2. A design decision you will defend that other people think is wrong.
3. What the film and media background actually gave you that a design degree
   would not have.
4. The part of this work you would do for free.
5. What you want to be doing in three years, said to a friend, not a panel.

## After each session

Do not listen back before transcribing. Run the pipeline first. Read the
transcript cold. The gap between what you remember saying and what is on the
page is the size of the problem you are trying to fix.

## The separate fix for the room

The rig above captures your voice. It does not solve the freeze. Those are
different problems and the second one has a simpler answer.

Bullet points fail under pressure because they are indexes into memory, and
pressure is exactly when the index lookup fails. Stories do not fail the same
way, because a story you have actually lived is one retrieval, not twenty.

Carry three stories, not twenty bullets. Each one concrete enough to have a room,
a screen, and a person in it. Almost every process question is answerable by
telling one of them and then saying what it taught you. When the question comes
and the answer is gone, you say: "Let me give you a specific one," and then you
start describing a place. Description restarts speech when abstraction cannot.
